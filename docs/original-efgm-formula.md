# Original EFGM Formula

## Historical formulation

The original Entropy-Flow Governance Model (EFGM) conceptual expression is preserved here **without retrospective rewriting**:

```text
T × E = Et ~ F ± e = A|M
```

Conceptually, this overall relationship was used to describe **coherence** in an adaptive system.

| Symbol | Original meaning |
|---|---|
| `T` | Time |
| `E` | Energy / Capability |
| `Et` | Energy Transfer |
| `F` | Flow |
| `e` | Entropy |
| `A` | Alignment / stable outcome |
| `M` | Misalignment / degraded outcome |

## Interpretation boundary

This document records the historical hypothesis; it does not assert that the expression is a validated physical law or conventional algebraic equality.

The notation is intentionally preserved:

- `T × E = Et`
- `Et ~ F`
- `F ± e = A|M`

The `~` should not be silently replaced with `=` or `→`. Likewise, the original expression should not be rewritten to incorporate later EFGM variables or experimental mechanisms.

## Relationship to later EFGM work

EFGM v1, EFGM v2 Decision Integrity, Agent Governance v0.3, entropy-recovery metrics, governance-integrity measurements, temporal governance, and artificial-homeostasis experiments are **later operationalizations, extensions, or experimental hypotheses**. They do not replace or retroactively modify the original formulation.

Later research may test whether mechanisms such as regulation, recovery, uncertainty handling, or governance integrity explain dynamics represented abstractly by `F ± e`. Such mappings must be treated as hypotheses requiring falsification rather than as meanings that were present in the original formula.

## Scientific provenance rule

When repository material discusses the history of EFGM, it should distinguish among:

1. **Original formulation** — `T × E = Et ~ F ± e = A|M`.
2. **Later interpretation** — conceptual mappings proposed after the original formulation.
3. **Operational model** — versioned equations and metrics implemented in EFGM v1/v2 or experimental extensions.
4. **Experimental evidence** — observations supported by preregistered or controlled experiments.

This separation prevents later results from being used to rewrite the hypothesis that preceded them.
